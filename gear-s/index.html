Navigation for Samsung Gear S

From customer proposal to product release

HERE partnered with Samsung to bring its maps and location platform services to the newly-announced Samsung Gear S and subsequently to all Samsung's Tizen-powered smart devices.

This partnership not only brought HERE a multi-million dollar deal, but also broadened its reach to users and businesses across screens and operating systems.

Challenge

Design and ship a navigation app in three months — for a device that didn't exist yet. The hardware and software were being built in parallel, by two separate companies, with no template for what wearable navigation should even look like.

Approach

Working in daily sync with Samsung's development team to translate HERE's maps and location platform into a glance-able wearable experience — rethinking navigation for a new form factor from the ground up.

Outcome

HERE for Gear launched at IFA 2014 as the first wearable navigation application of its kind, securing a multi-million dollar deal and leading to HERE maps being preinstalled across all Samsung Tizen-powered devices.

Role

Led all stages of product design including ideation, identification, evaluation, pitch case, UI/UX, IA, product strategy, visual design, user research and launch.

Winning the partnership

Samsung had announced the Gear S — a wearable with a curved screen and a data connection of its own. HERE saw an opportunity to bring its maps and location platform to a device category that had no serious navigation experience yet. Winning the partnership meant building a compelling enough product vision to close a multimillion dollar deal, then delivering it in three months alongside a device that was still being built.

Building in parallel

The hardest part wasn't designing for a new form factor. It was that the device itself didn't exist yet. The application and the hardware were being built simultaneously, by two companies, in two countries. That meant decisions made in Berlin had immediate consequences in Seoul — and vice versa. Daily software exchange kept both sides in sync, but it also meant the design had to be resilient enough to absorb constant change without losing coherence.

Designing for the wrist

A wearable navigation experience demands a different kind of clarity. Users glance at their wrist for two seconds while moving through the world — there's no room for hierarchy, no tolerance for ambiguity. Every screen was stripped back to the single most important piece of information for that moment. Turn-by-turn guidance, public transport routing, live traffic — all reimagined around a fundamentally different relationship between the user and the device.

Release 1

HERE for Gear launched at IFA 2014, three months from concept to release. It was the first application to offer turn-by-turn walk navigation and public transit routing on a wearable device — covering 100 countries for drive and walk guidance, 750+ cities across 40+ countries for public transport, and live traffic for 40+ countries.

Release 2 — going standalone

The second release removed the dependency on a paired phone entirely. Offline map storage meant users could navigate, search and get directions independently. New map styles adapted to context — public transport, pedestrian, night mode. The manoeuvre view was redesigned to show each instruction in the context of the surrounding route, giving users orientation rather than just a command.

Refining through testing

Throughout development, the interface was tested directly on the physical device — not on screen, not in simulation. Interaction patterns that worked in Figma sometimes failed at wrist scale, in motion, in real conditions. Iterative testing caught those gaps early and kept the experience honest.

The result

The product launched on time. The partnership secured a multi-million dollar deal and led to HERE maps being preinstalled across all Samsung Gear S and Tizen-powered mobile devices — extending HERE's reach well beyond the original brief and establishing a template for future platform partnerships.
